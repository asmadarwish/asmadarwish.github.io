About Me

Education and Health Are Intertwined

I grew up in Afghanistan, where cultural and systemic barriers kept many women and children from basic health education and preventive care. Today it is one of the few countries in the world that denies women both education and health care. Illness often went untreated, and topics like mental health and women's health were rarely discussed. These gaps come from living in long-term survival mode, where the full value of health is rarely recognized. Those experiences are why I chose public health, and why I'm drawn to epidemiology: understanding the patterns and causes of disease in communities shaped by poverty, conflict, and gender inequity.

During high school, before my academic career, I directed the Baale Parwaz Library for girls in Kabul, where more than seventy percent of families could not read. That work showed me that literacy is a social determinant of health, and that the surest way to improve community health is to reach women and mothers, who are often the primary caregivers.

I began my undergraduate studies at the American University of Beirut on a full scholarship from the Education for Leadership in Crisis program. In my sophomore year, I transferred to Hollins University, where I completed my BA in Public Health. I'm now pursuing an MPH in Infectious Disease Epidemiology at Cornell, where the One Health approach connects human, animal, and environmental health. My goal is to build evidence-based strategies that reduce health disparities for women, children, and marginalized populations.

Internship

NYU Langone Health, Department of Population Health · Research Intern, May 2026 to present

I helped prepare the evaluation tools for an upcoming study of AI mental health tools for youth, grounded in the NIMH Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) framework. Data collection has not started yet. My work focused on deciding how the study should measure whether these tools are usable and acceptable, and what behavioral and physiological signals they should track.

Deliverable 1 · Instrument

Usability, Acceptability & Feasibility Instrument for RDoC-Guided AI Mental Health Tools

Youth, caregiver, and provider versions

Reviewed 12 validated measures, then built a six-part instrument combining observed usability tasks, the System Usability Scale, AIM/IAM/FIM, 38 AI-specific items on passive sensing, trust, and prompt timing, and interview guides.

Available on request

Deliverable 2 · Framework

Measurement Framework: Passive and Behavioral Indicators Alongside Self-Report

Grounded in the NIMH Research Domain Criteria

Maps each RDoC domain to passive phone and wearable indicators, pairs each with a validated self-report anchor, and phases them by evidence, feasibility, privacy risk, and acceptability. Recommends starting with sleep and physical activity.

Grant proposal

TickSafe Finger Lakes Initiative

VTPEH 6182 Public Health Practice: Planning · Spring 2026 · with Hana Bauta and Leslie Flora

A one-year, community-based pilot to reduce tick-borne disease in the Finger Lakes, where Lyme disease incidence in New York State rose from 37.2 to 164.8 cases per 100,000 between 2020 and 2024. Grounded in Social Cognitive Theory, it replaces passive information campaigns with active outreach at schools, parks, trailheads, and farmers markets, and builds partnerships with Cornell Cooperative Extension, Cornell IPM, Cayuga Health, and county health departments.

Budget request of $14,313 ($19,913 with in-kind support), evaluated through pre/post surveys, attendance, and partnership tracking.

Available on request

Data analysis · R

Telehealth, Pharmacist Prescribing, and Paxlovid Availability at U.S. COVID-19 Treatment Sites

VTPEH 6270 · Spring 2026 · Individual project

An analysis of 64,237 provider sites in the HHS COVID-19 Treatment Locator. Telehealth sites were far more likely to stock Paxlovid (88.2% vs. 18.1%, unadjusted OR 33.8), but the association was no longer significant after adjusting for pharmacist prescribing authority, which nearly always occurred alongside telehealth.

Methods: descriptive statistics, chi-squared and Fisher's exact tests, unadjusted and adjusted logistic regression, and a simulation-based power analysis.
